# 1. Executive summary

Multi-hop question answering over tables needs a sequence of path-dependent choices: which cell to visit, which linked passage to read, when to stop. Reinforcement-learning approaches to table reasoning usually reward only the final answer, so no individual hop receives credit. We study **TableHop-PPO**, which treats every hop as a discrete entity-selection action and trains the selection policy with PPO using a hybrid reward: a sparse answer reward plus dense per-hop terms (on-path hop, redundancy penalty, schema-consistency penalty, step cost).

We compare this method against supervised fine-tuning (B1), trace-level GRPO with an answer-only reward (B2a), trace-level GRPO with a gold-path-aware terminal reward that acts as a supervision control (B2b), and a soft region-selection model (B3). All path-based methods share one frozen encoder, one candidate generator and one frozen answer head, so that only the training paradigm differs. Four ablations (A1 to A4) isolate the dense reward and the action-space restriction. The study runs on HybridQA with whole-table-cluster held-out splits for out-of-domain evaluation, under a compute budget of one Apple M4 laptop (16 GB, no GPU) over roughly three weeks.

This report documents the problem, related work, method, implementation, how the work was organized, the compute actually used, deviations from the proposal, risks we met, and limitations. Final result tables and figures are inserted automatically from `results/` by `scripts/make_report.py`. Numbers in the text refer to the {{SPLIT}} split and to the seeds completed at build time ({{SeedsHOneHoppo}} per method in this build).

**Headline results ({{SPLIT}} split, {{SeedsHOneHoppo}} seeds).** Full-table SFT (B1) is the most accurate method with {{EmBOneSft}}% exact match, against {{EmHOneHoppo}}% for Hop-PPO (H1); giving the frozen answer head the gold path (M0) reaches {{EmMZero}}%, so choosing evidence is the bottleneck. Hop-PPO beats GRPO that gets the same path signal only at the end (B2b: {{DiffBTwoBGrpoPath}} points, 95% CI [{{DiffloBTwoBGrpoPath}}, {{DiffhiBTwoBGrpoPath}}], positive on every seed) and has the most valid paths among the main methods ({{ValidHOneHoppo}}%), but it is not better than answer-only GRPO (B2a: {{DiffBTwoAGrpo}} points; per seed {{SeeddiffBTwoAGrpo}}), and the dense hop reward does not improve accuracy over a terminal-only reward. Across seeds, policies that learn to stop after about two hops read less evidence and answer fewer questions.

# 2. Problem and objectives

## 2.1 Problem

A multi-hop table question cannot be answered by looking at a single cell. A worked example (illustrative, made up, not taken from any dataset): "In which city is the team of the top scorer based?" Hop 1 selects, in a Players table, the row with the most goals; hop 2 reads the team cell of that row; hop 3 finds that team in a Teams table and reads its city. Trace-level RL gives no signal on hops 1 and 2 and a reward only when the final answer appears. If the answer is wrong, the learner cannot tell which hop failed. This is the credit-assignment gap that motivates hop-level training.

## 2.2 Research question and hypotheses

Can multi-hop table reasoning be formulated as a sequential entity-selection decision process in which each hop is a credit-assignable PPO action, and does this improve answer accuracy, path validity and out-of-domain generalization over trace-level rewards and soft region selection? Our pre-registered hypotheses (docs/PROPOSAL.md, tracked in docs/CLAIMS.md) are:

- **H1.** Hop-level credit reduces error propagation relative to trace-level rewards (B2a and B2b).
- **H1b.** Gains are not simply the result of extra supervision (comparison with B2b).
- **H1c.** The dense reward, not only the discrete actions, matters (comparison with A1).
- **H2.** Selected paths are auditable and valid.
- **H3.** The drop from in-domain to out-of-domain accuracy is smaller than for SFT and trace-level RL.

## 2.3 Objectives

1. Build a modular, tested code base that a third party can rebuild from scripts (data, caches, answer head, all methods, evaluation, tables, figures, paper, report, slides).
2. Compare methods under matched compute, matched tuning budget and identical encoder, candidate generator and answer head.
3. Report negative results as they are. A hypothesis whose evidence does not clear the criteria in docs/CLAIMS.md is reported as not supported or inconclusive.

# 3. Literature review

All references below were verified by fetching the source page and checking title, authors, venue and year (docs/lit/LITERATURE.md, 24 entries, matching `paper/refs.bib`). Two anchors (MA-TPath, MASKGR) are verified through bibliographic metadata only; their method details were behind a paywall, so we cite them for what the metadata supports and do not describe their internals.

**Table reasoning with language models.** TAPAS (Herzig et al., 2020) and TAPEX (Liu et al., 2022) pre-train table encoders for weakly supervised parsing and neural SQL execution. TableLlama (Zhang et al., 2024) instruction-tunes a generalist model for table tasks. Prompting methods decompose or program the reasoning: Binder (Cheng et al., 2023), Dater (Ye et al., 2023) and Chain-of-Table (Wang et al., 2024). These systems either supervise the final answer or rely on prompting and do not train a hop-by-hop selection policy.

**RL for table reasoning.** Table-R1 (Yang et al., 2025) applies reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (GRPO-style) and reports gains on multi-step and out-of-domain table tasks, with a reward over the whole trace. Reasoning-Table (Lei et al., 2025) also explores RL for table reasoning. GRPO itself is described in DeepSeekMath (Shao et al., 2024). These works motivate our trace-level baselines B2a and B2b, which we implement inside our own controlled setting rather than reproduce.

**Multi-hop reasoning with RL on knowledge graphs.** DeepPath (Xiong et al., 2017), MINERVA (Das et al., 2018) and reward-shaped multi-hop reasoning (Lin et al., 2018) walk a graph with a policy and reward arrival at the answer. MA-TPath (Bai et al., 2024) and MASKGR (Liu et al., 2025) use cooperating relation and entity agents on temporal and sparse knowledge graphs. Tables have rows, columns and cells instead of triples, so these agents cannot be reused unchanged. To our knowledge, and based on our search, we found no published work doing hop-level PPO entity selection on tables; this is a statement about our search, not a proof of absence.

**Credit assignment and reward design.** Process-level feedback improves over outcome-only feedback in mathematical reasoning (Uesato et al., 2022; Lightman et al., 2023). Potential-based shaping (Ng et al., 1999) shows when shaping terms leave optimal policies unchanged; our per-hop reward is not potential-based, and we do not claim policy invariance. RUDDER (Arjona-Medina et al., 2018) decomposes delayed returns. Our optimiser is PPO (Schulman et al., 2017) with generalized advantage estimation (Schulman et al., 2015).

**Datasets.** HybridQA (Chen et al., 2020) is our primary benchmark. MultiHiertt (Zhao et al., 2022), TAT-QA (Zhu et al., 2021) and FinQA (Chen et al., 2021) were in the plan and were cut for scope (Section 10).

**Gap.** Hop-level, PPO-optimized entity selection over tables, compared against trace-level RL with a supervision control and against soft selection under matched compute, is the combination we study.

# 4. Methodology

## 4.1 Formulation

Each question is an episode in a partially observable decision process. The state contains the question, the trace of entities selected so far and the candidate set reachable from the last selected entity. An action selects the next entity (a cell or a linked passage) or STOP. After at most four hops (or STOP) the frozen answer head reads the selected evidence and writes the answer, from which the terminal reward is computed.

![Figure 1. Pipeline of the method. State (question, trace, candidates) goes to the policy, which selects an entity; the table environment checks the hop and returns the hop reward and next state; on STOP the frozen answer head produces the answer and the terminal reward.](docs/report/F1_pipeline.png){width=6in}

## 4.2 Candidates and encoder

The question and every entity are encoded by a frozen `BAAI/bge-small-en-v1.5` model (384 dimensions), cached on disk. At hop 0 the candidates are the top 32 cells by a mix of BM25 and cosine similarity. At later hops, from entity *e*, the candidates are cells in the same row, the top same-column cells by cosine, the passage linked from *e* (if any) and STOP. Each candidate carries a relation type (start, same row, same column, link, stop) and four scalar features (cosine, normalized BM25, question-token overlap, header overlap; design change R2 in Section 10). Illegal actions are masked. Recall of the first gold entity within the hop-0 candidates bounds achievable accuracy and is reported below.

## 4.3 Policy and value

The policy is a small network (about 0.3 M parameters, run on CPU). A GRU over the embeddings of the selected entities, initialised from the question, yields the state vector. The logit of each candidate is a bilinear term between state and candidate embedding, plus a relation-type term, a learned-scale question-candidate cosine term and a feature term. The value head is an MLP on the state vector. Since the encoder is frozen, the trainable part is deliberately small.

## 4.4 Hybrid reward

Per hop *t*, before termination: reward `w_hop` if the entity lies on the gold path in an order-consistent way (a gold entity revisited does not earn it again), minus `w_red` if the entity was already visited, minus `w_step`. At termination: `w_ans` times the answer F1, minus `w_schema` if the last selected cell has a data type incompatible with the type implied by question cues ("how many", "what year", and so on). Defaults are `w_ans=1.0, w_hop=0.2, w_red=0.1, w_schema=0.05, w_step=0.02`, fixed before any tuning. Examples without a derivable gold path receive no hop term; their fraction is reported in the data section.

## 4.5 PPO and the trace-level baselines

H1 uses PPO with generalized advantage estimation (lambda 0.95, gamma 1), clip 0.2, value coefficient 0.5, entropy coefficient 0.01 with linear decay, four epochs per batch, gradient clipping at 0.5. GRPO baselines (B2a, B2b) share the policy, candidates and rollout code but sample groups of eight trajectories per question, normalize rewards within the group and apply the advantage to every action of a trajectory. B2a rewards answer F1 only. B2b adds 0.2 times the path set-F1 at the end of the trajectory, so it uses gold-path labels without any per-hop credit; this isolates credit assignment from extra supervision.

## 4.6 Frozen answer head and evidence rendering

The answer head is `google/flan-t5-small`, fine-tuned once on gold evidence (with random distractors inserted at rate 0.3 to tolerate imperfect selections) and then frozen. Following design change R3, a selected cell is rendered as its whole row and a selected passage as its text; passages are visible only if the policy selects them. All path-based methods load the identical checkpoint, whose hash is recorded in each run's `meta.json`. M0 is the oracle: the same head fed gold evidence, giving the ceiling for path-based methods.

| ID | Method | Role |

## 4.8 Metrics and statistics

- **Exact match (EM)** and **token F1** after SQuAD-style normalization (lowercase, strip articles and punctuation), maximum over gold answers.
- **Strict path validity**: fraction of selected hops that are legal, non-redundant and on the gold path; computed only on examples with a gold path. **Hop efficiency**: minimum hops divided by hops taken, set to zero for an incorrect answer. **Mean hops** is the average trajectory length.
- **ID to OOD gap**: in-domain EM minus out-of-domain EM, absolute and relative.
- Statistics: 95% bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 resamples over questions) and paired bootstrap tests of H1 against each method on the same questions. Seed variability is reported as the standard deviation over seeds together with the number of seeds. We do not claim significance from seed-level tests with fewer than five seeds.

# 7. Experimental setup and compute

## 7.1 Data and splits

We use HybridQA (Chen et al., 2020). The official test file is never opened. All splits are derived from the official train and dev files. Tables are clustered by k-means (k=20, seed 0, fitted on train tables) over embeddings of title, section title and header; three whole clusters (6, 16, 19) are held out as out-of-domain schemas. Splits are: `train` (3,000 questions, a seeded subset stratified by answer-node type), `dev` (1,000 questions, tables disjoint from train, used for all tuning), `test` (official-dev questions on in-domain tables unused by train and dev), `ood_test` (official-dev questions on held-out clusters) and `ood_dev` (diagnostics only). `scripts/audit_leakage.py` checks table-identifier disjointness across all pairs and cluster disjointness for the out-of-domain splits, and must pass. Test and out-of-domain test are evaluated once per final run, and the evaluation script refuses a second evaluation.

Gold paths are heuristic. HybridQA annotates an answer node but no hop order, so a path is the answer node plus an anchor cell in the same row chosen by lexical overlap with the question. ## 7.3 Matched budget and tuning

The budget unit is the number of (question, trajectory) rollouts processed in training: 48,000 for each trained method (for B1 and B3, 48,000 training examples, equal to 16 passes over the 3,000 training questions). GRPO counts every sampled trajectory. Tuning uses three trials per trained method on dev with seed 0 (design change R4): RL methods try the default, a higher head learning rate and a higher entropy coefficient; B3 and B1 try three learning rates each. The ablations inherit the choice made for H1. Reward weights stay as pre-registered. The selected configuration is run with three seeds (0, 1, 2); the number of seeds actually completed is shown in every results table.

# 9. Analysis and discussion

Hop-level credit helped where path supervision is concerned: H1 beats trace-level RL that receives the same path signal only at the end (H1b supported) and has the most valid paths among the main methods. It did not beat answer-only GRPO (H1 not supported), the dense reward did not improve accuracy (H1c not supported), H2 is partially supported and H3 is inconclusive. The oracle M0 ({{EmMZero}}%) shows the reader is not the limit; candidate recall ({{RecallKDev}}%), the first hop and the amount of evidence collected are.

Possible reasons for the gap to SFT (not tested separately): B1 reads the whole table plus passages at once, while path methods see only 1-4 selected entities; the encoder is small and frozen; gold paths are heuristic ({{GoldCovTrain}}% coverage, answer string in the last entity for {{LastEntAnsTrain}}%); the reader is a 77M-parameter model; and training uses 3,000 questions.

B2b receives the same gold-path information as H1 but only as one bonus at the end. H1 beats it on every seed ({{DiffBTwoBGrpoPath}} points), so how path credit is distributed over hops matters, not only whether path labels are used. At the same time, answer-only GRPO (B2a), with no path labels, is at least as accurate as H1, so path supervision itself did not raise accuracy.

# 10. Deviations from the proposal

The following are decisions recorded in `STATUS.md`. All were taken on dev data or before any test evaluation, and none touches the test split.

**Scale (compute tier S).** The proposal planned a 7B-class backbone on one or two 40 to 80 GB GPUs, with a 1.5B to 3B fallback. We had a laptop with no GPU. We therefore freeze a small encoder and answer head and train only a small policy, which changes what "PPO on a language model" means: our policy chooses among candidate entities and does not generate text.

**Scope cuts.** Kept: HybridQA, H1, B1, B2a, B2b, B3, A1 to A4, metrics with bootstrap, three seeds, all deliverables, a synthetic sanity study. Cut: MultiHiertt (only if time allowed), the dual-agent knowledge-graph baseline B4, text-generating GRPO B5, TAT-QA and FinQA, five seeds, larger tiers, and the potential-based shaping ablation A5 (not in the implementation contract). Training uses up to 3,000 HybridQA training questions.

**Out-of-domain protocol.** Cross-dataset transfer (finance to sports and similar) was replaced by held-out table clusters inside HybridQA, so "out of domain" means unseen table topics and schemas within one dataset, which is a weaker test than the proposal's.

**Reproduction target (R1).** No published HybridQA numbers exist for our model sizes, splits and path-based setting. R1 was changed from a reproduction target to "report published numbers for context only"; our numbers are not comparable to published HybridQA systems.

**R2 (candidate features, 2026-11-23).** The first real H1 run, before R2, reached a low dev EM, and dev EM on a fixed dev subset fell during training. Diagnostics on 300 dev questions (dev only, test untouched) showed that behaviour cloning on gold paths reached only a low first-hop accuracy, even on training questions, below a BM25-argmax heuristic. The gold anchor is lexical and the policy saw only embeddings, while B3 already received cosine and BM25 scalars. The fix adds four per-candidate features, applied identically to H1, A1 to A4, B2a and B2b (policy) and to B3 (scorer). All earlier RL runs are superseded and excluded from results.

**R3 (row evidence, 2026-11-23).** The answer head now reads whole rows for selected cells and passage text for selected passages, applied to every path method including the oracle. A non-learned reference (top first-hop cell plus its row's linked passages) was measured on 300 dev questions as context for what a simple heuristic can reach with this head.

**R4 (tuning budget, 2026-11-24).** Three trials per method instead of the specification's six, a fixed grid, due to the time budget. This is equal across methods, so the fairness protocol is kept, but the tuning is lighter than planned.

**Minor.** Embedding inputs are truncated at 160 tokens for speed on MPS. The hop-0 candidate count is 32 (the specification draft said 64) with a same-column cap of eight. The time budget was set by the user on 2026-11-13 as two to three weeks.

# 14. Limitations and future work

- **Compute scale.** Everything runs on a laptop with frozen small models. Our results say nothing about 7B-class backbones or LLM-generated reasoning, which is where Table-R1 operates.
- **Weak labels.** All gold paths are heuristic and unordered in the source data, so hop-level rewards and path metrics measure agreement with a heuristic path, not with human-validated reasoning. A policy can be penalised for a legitimate alternative route.
- **One dataset family and a proxy for OOD.** Only HybridQA is used; out-of-domain means held-out table clusters in the same dataset. Findings may not transfer to numerical or hierarchical financial tables.
- **Single backbone family.** The encoder is bge-small and the answer head is flan-t5-small for every method; no other backbone was tried.
- **Small training set and few seeds.** Training uses at most 3,000 questions; three seeds give wide uncertainty and limit seed-level claims.
- **Light tuning.** Three trials per method on a fixed grid (R4).
- **Answer head as a bound.** Accuracy of every path-based method is bounded by candidate recall and by the frozen answer head.
- **Synthetic study.** Used for tests and a labelled sanity check only; never a benchmark.
- **Agent-assisted development.** The code base and drafts were produced largely by language-model agents under human specification; all numbers are traceable to `results/`, but residual errors in code remain possible despite the tests.

Future work, in the order we would do it: complete the cut datasets (MultiHiertt, TAT-QA), human-validated gold paths for a subset, a larger backbone with LoRA on a GPU, a potential-based shaping variant, and a knowledge-graph-style dual-agent baseline.
